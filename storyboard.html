Target Spec · v4

BSC AI Career Coach

A chat-based tech career coaching assistant for Ascendency. The coach's 57 training questions are grouped into ten discussion areas; a conversation enters one area, stays inside it for as long as it's useful, then deliberately closes it before opening another — so the user works through one subject at a time instead of receiving scattered one-off answers.

This is a target spec, not a description of the deployed bot. Up to v3 this document recorded what the code already did. From v4 it also records what we want it to do — the area-based conversation model below is a design request, not shipped behaviour. Overview · State

Area Lifecycle — Enter, Stay, Leave PROPOSED

This replaces the old two-state model (no-context → context-captured). The difference that matters: being inside an area is a state the bot holds and can leave, not just a tag on the last answer. A conversation should be able to work through salary properly, close it off, and then open mentorship as a fresh subject — with the coach aware that salary was already covered.

STATE A · Outside any area

Opening messages, or the moment just after an area was closed.

  • Greeting and out-of-scope handled as they are today
  • A message pointing clearly at one area enters it directly
  • A broad ask gets a narrowing question whose options are the areas themselves
  • Returning users are offered areas they haven't covered yet
STATE B · Inside an area

One area is open. This is where most of the conversation happens.

  • Answers are drawn from that area's knowledge and its questions only
  • Follow-ups are read as belonging to this area unless clearly not
  • The coach can offer a natural next facet from the same area
  • Facets covered are recorded, so nothing is repeated on return
  • An off-area question triggers the exit check rather than a silent jump
STATE C · Closing the area

An explicit hand-off, so a switch is never accidental.

  • Triggered by the user asking to move on, or an off-area question
  • Also offered by the coach once an area's main facets are covered
  • Briefly sums up what was covered before closing
  • Then offers the remaining areas — returning to State A
  • Re-entering a closed area resumes it rather than starting over
The gap to close: today the only stored context is career_topic — a single overwritable field with no memory of what was already discussed, and no concept of an area being open or closed. See ISSUE-008 and ISSUE-009.

Inside Salary & Negotiation PROPOSED

What happens during STATE B · Inside an area. Every answer ends with a question, and that question hands off to another facet we already have an answer for — so the conversation is led rather than left open. This is the first of ten; the other nine follow the same shape once this one is agreed.

Read this before the diagrams: the graph is a coverage map, not a router. An earlier draft of this page specified deterministic facet-to-facet routing — S1 leads to S2, S2 leads to S3. That was wrong, and wrong in a way this project has been caught by before: every deterministic layer in this coach has been outgrown, from isBroadStartingAsk to the keyword pre-check that rule 5 replaced. A fifteen-node edge table is a keyword regex in better clothes, and it breaks the first time someone says something that sits between two facets.

What actually happens inside an area: for every user message, one classification call places them in a stage — or finds they are leaving. The stage decides which of Otema's answers are injected as few-shot grounding, and the model writes a fresh reply from that material. Structure comes from the stage; the words come from the model. This is the wordalisation pattern the coach already uses in loadFewShotExamples(), keyed on stage instead of topic — so it is a smaller change than a router, not a bigger one.

So the facets and edges below record what material each stage can draw on. They are never consulted to decide what to say.
Seven examples per reply: the three closest, plus four drawn at random from the area. CHANGED Selecting only the nearest matches couples the answers too tightly to the examples — retrieval by word overlap returns examples that resemble each other as much as they resemble the question, so the model hears one shape and gives it back. The random four widen the voice it is matching: it hears her across the whole area, blunt in places and softer in others, rather than echoing the nearest neighbour.

The two sets are labelled differently in the prompt, and that label is doing the real work. The close three are voice reference for this question. The random four are introduced as "OTHER ANSWERS OF YOURS, on different questions in this same area. These are NOT about what she asked and you must not answer from them… Take the range, leave the content." Without that, the model treats a far-off example as material and tries to work it in. Only the close three are recorded as covered — the random four were shown, not drawn on, and marking them used would burn through an area in two turns.

When nothing fits at all. Retrieval returns its full quota however badly the examples match. Measured against the salary pool, a well-matched question scores 4 against its best example; "should they cover relocation and visa fees" scores 1, and "what laptop should I buy" scores 0 — and all three previously produced the same confident-looking prompt. When the best match scores 1 or less the framing now changes outright: "Nothing here is close to what she just asked, so take ONLY the voice… Do not bend her question towards these answers because they are what you have."

Repeat examples are allowed on purpose. A used example is down-weighted, not removed, so the same answer can surface again in a later turn. Some repetition is right — a point worth making once is often worth returning to — and the guards against restating a reply verbatim sit further downstream, in dropRepeatedSentences() and the do-not-repeat-yourself rules.
What the mapping bought us. All 31 answers already end in a question, and several of those closers already point at another facet. Mapping them found the 16 replies with nothing behind them — which is why the drafted pool exists. That work stands: it is what tells you whether a stage can actually carry a conversation. It just isn't a route.

What Happens After the Question PROPOSED

The level below the facet graph. Each closing question is a decision point, and the user's reply decides what comes next — but a reply only leads somewhere if we have an answer waiting. Rows shaded amber are replies with nothing behind them: content that has to be written or asked of Otema before the flow works.

Two kinds of closing question, and only one of them branches. REFINING closers gather detail to give a better version of the same answer — "What role and location?" doesn't move anywhere, it sharpens where you already are. BRANCHING closers genuinely fork the conversation, and those are the ones that need an answer per branch. Mistaking one for the other is how you end up with a question that leads nowhere.

G5

Freelance & contract rates

DRAFTED

How do I set my rate as a freelancer or contractor?

Work out what you need to earn in a year, then divide by realistic billable days — not 250, more like 150 once you account for finding the work and doing your own admin. That gives you a floor, not a target. Then check what the client's market pays, because an agency in Lagos and a client in Berlin are two different rate cards for identical work. Undercharging at the start is very hard to walk back with the same client. Who's your first client likely to be?

G6

Equity instead of salary

DRAFTED

A startup offered me equity instead of a higher salary. How do I know what it's worth?

Ask three things before you value it at anything: what percentage of the whole company is it, what's the vesting schedule, and has anyone actually sold shares yet. If they won't answer the first one, treat the equity as zero and negotiate on cash. I've watched too many people take a real pay cut for paper that never converted, especially at companies with no clear path to raising again. What stage is this company at?

DRAFTED

Get the payment method written into the contract as well, because "we'll sort out transfers later" quietly becomes your problem, not theirs. Check who carries the transfer fees too. DRAFTED

Unpaid or low-paid first role

DRAFTED

Should I take an unpaid or very low-paid role to get my first bit of experience?

Only if it's time-boxed and written down — what you'll learn, what you'll have built by the end, and the date it either ends or converts to paid.
